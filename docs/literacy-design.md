# Kindergarten literacy game design

The listening games are designed so a child does not need to read directions. Each round speaks the task automatically, the speaker button repeats it, and picture choices are named aloud.

## Instruction pattern

- Round 1 models the skill: the narrator says the target, demonstrates the matching answer, and asks the child to tap it.
- Rounds 2 and 3 provide independent practice with a new item.
- Answer positions are shuffled every round.
- An incorrect choice is named, followed immediately by the complete listening prompt again.
- Ending- and middle-sound items use familiar one-syllable CVC words and pictures.
- Rhyming starts with familiar word pairs and repeats both words aloud.
- High-frequency words are introduced by saying the word, spelling it, and saying it again before recognition practice.

## Research alignment

- Common Core RF.K.2.d: isolate and pronounce initial, medial-vowel, and final phonemes in three-phoneme CVC words: https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/2/d/
- Common Core RF.K.3.c: recognize common high-frequency words by sight: https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/K/3/c/
- U.S. Institute of Education Sciences, *Foundational Skills to Support Reading for Understanding in Kindergarten Through 3rd Grade*: explicit modeling; movement from larger to smaller sound units; linking speech sounds to letters; immediate practice; and explicit instruction for regular and irregular high-frequency words: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21

These activities are short practice experiences, not a reading assessment or replacement for instruction with an adult.
